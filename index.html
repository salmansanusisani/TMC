The Main Characters

Chapter 1: Cringe Meeting

It was a day in school. All senior secondary students were writing their final year examinations called WAEC/NECO — the West African Examinations Council exam carried out in several African countries every year. Among those millions of students was a 17-year-old student named Salman.

As Salman and his classmates were writing WAEC, new students joined them. They weren’t from the school — just external candidates. Among them was a male candidate and a female candidate. From the moment the female student arrived, Salman noticed her immediately. It’s natural to notice someone new in your surroundings. At first, it was just curiosity.

If you were to ask Salman to describe her, he would say she was a young, very neat lady around 18 or 19 years old. She appeared to be a black beauty, slightly chubby, always neat and flashy. Without a doubt, he knew she was a 10/10. But she wasn’t his type. He acknowledged her beauty, but he didn’t view her as someone he could ever fall for. According to him, her class was far above his — but he didn’t care much. Still, something in him wanted to be friends with her. She looked intimidating, but he wasn’t really intimidated.

One day in the exam hall, before the exam began, Salman and his friend Musa were passing time by quizzing each other on the 2011 anime Hunter x Hunter. Whoever answered first got a point, and the first to reach 10 points won. The loser had to buy the winner a drink. After a heated round, Salman turned out victorious, so Musa owed him a drink after the exam.

Feeling playful, Salman dared Musa to talk to the newcomer. Musa refused, even when offered 5k. Instead, Musa flipped the dare back on Salman. Salman paused — “Is today my lucky day? Free 5k plus a free drink, and I already wanted to talk to her.” So he agreed.

But Musa added a condition: Salman had to approach her with a cringe pickup line — smooth only if executed correctly. He even showed Salman the pickup line video on his phone. Risking his reputation for 5k, Salman went straight to where she was sitting, reading a book, appeared right in front of her, and said, "Where did you get that?" She looked at Salman very confused and was like, "Huh??" Salman was supposed to finish the pickup line by saying, "Where did you get that beauty?" so she would smile and everything would go as he imagined. But suddenly, a lot of thoughts started flooding his mind: What if it looks weird when I say it? What if she laughs at me? This froze his brain for seconds, making him look like a statue, and she also looked at him like, "What? I'm listening." He couldn't stand the pressure and searched for a smooth way to get out of this trap, but after his mini calculations, he realized there was no way out. He just had to say it. So he finished the pickup line, saying, "Where did you get that beauty?" She looked at him in a weird way, thinking, "What kind of cringe guy is this?" Salman knew at that moment he had just messed up and lost aura in front of this new girl. He tried to act a little cool by asking her name. He said, "What's your name?" She replied, "Khadija." He said, "Okay, nice to meet you," and left with a fake smile on his face, trying to convince himself: "This is just a newcomer who came to write exams with us. When we're done, I'm never going to see her again, so just forget this ever happened." With that, he was able to convince himself and move on.

After the cringe incident, he went back to his friend Musa, who was shocked to see that Salman completed the challenge. After the exams, Salman and Musa went to a nearby mini-mart in the school, where Musa bought him the drink as promised and transferred the 5k to his account without any argument, knowing how rough it went. At last, Salman felt some relief, but that memory still lowkey haunted him. He thought, "So there is a girl who’s going to see me as a cringy person now." He knew the experience was not the best, and worst of all, what if she told other girls? Salman was going to lose more aura. He lowkey accepted his destiny and moved on. So every time he saw her, he would just say hi like they had met before and chat, so they were no longer strangers. She always replied with a smile, like a normal person would, as if nothing had ever happened.

Under normal circumstances, Salman would never do that, but he was kind of a risk-taker, so he somehow learned a new lesson. The only thing that made the first meeting cringe was that Salman went in with full confidence, overthought it, and lost that confidence, resulting in the awkward moment he had with her. This is a moment that can never be forgotten, so he named the incident "THE AURA GAMBIT." He calls it the aura gambit because he sacrificed all the risk to either lose aura on the pickup line or gain aura, which turned out negative. In the game of chess, players sacrifice a valuable piece in order to gain something big later on, which is known as a gambit.

Chapter 2: Unexpected Bond

After the Aura Gambit incident, the exam period was nearing its end, with only two papers remaining. Salman was a talented artist who loved drawing. Whenever he finished an exam early, instead of sitting and waiting for time to pass, he sketched on his question paper to keep busy.

One day in the exam hall, he was drawing Ryomen Sukuna from Jujutsu Kaisen when a female classmate picked up another student’s question paper and showed it to him. On it was a drawing of a beautiful dress. The moment Salman saw it, his excitement spiked. Who is this good? I need to know. I want to challenge whoever drew this. Without wasting time, he asked whose drawing it was. She pointed to the newcomer, Khadija.

Trying to play it cool, Salman said, “I like your drawing.” She replied, “Thank you.” He followed up with, “Since we’re both done with our exams, let’s have a drawing contest to see who’s the best.” Khadija accepted immediately, as if the awkward first encounter had never happened. They both got to work, turning their question papers into mini–masterpieces. Salman felt relieved; her willingness to accept the challenge meant she either didn’t see the earlier incident as a big deal or didn’t even recognize him as the same guy.

When time was up and students began to leave, Salman went over to Khadija. She was adding final touches, then smiled and said, “Let me see yours.” He showed her his Sukuna drawing. There was one complication — their styles were completely different. Salman’s art was anime-style, while Khadija’s leaned into fashion design, which made judging fairly a challenge. One of Khadija’s friends offered to judge. Unsurprisingly, the girls preferred the fashion illustration and picked Khadija as the winner. Deep down, Salman still believed his piece was stronger, but he also knew they might not fully appreciate his style. Khadija then brought out her phone and took pictures of both drawings.

In the end, both Salman and Khadija walked away feeling like winners. That moment marked the start of their unexpected bond—art was the bridge that connected them. The next day was the last day of their exams, so they prepared for their final drawing challenge. As soon as they finished their papers, both got to work. Salman knew she didn’t stand a chance against him, but he still played along. That day Salman was drawing one of his favorite anime characters, Roronoa Zoro from the famous anime series One Piece, while Khadija was drawing another dress, meticulously shading and adding all the details it needed to stand out.

As the exam ended and everyone submitted their booklets, Salman went to Khadija and said, “Let me see yours.” Khadija playfully turned her question paper upside down and said, “No, I’m not done. Let me see yours first.” Salman placed his paper on her desk, and she smiled before finally turning hers over to show him. The same problem occurred — their drawing styles were different, making it hard to judge. Once again, the girls picked Khadija’s as the better one, but they all agreed Salman’s drawing had crazy details. Salman wasn’t satisfied, so he called his friend Musa, who without hesitation said Salman’s was better. Still, that didn’t convince the others, so Salman just played along. Deep down, he knew he was the real winner.

After their last meeting, Salman and Khadija left the school as art-challenge friends. Salman didn’t imagine they’d meet again, so he forgot about her. But fate had other plans. While scrolling on Instagram, Salman saw a familiar face in his account suggestions — it was Khadija. Excited, he clicked on the account, confirmed it was her, and followed her, hoping she’d follow back. He even sent her a reel about artists to get her attention, but days passed and she didn’t notice.

Luckily, the school later called all the exam candidates for a meeting. Only a few students showed up, including Salman and Khadija. After the meeting, as everyone gathered outside, Khadija stood by herself using her phone. Salman, who was nearby with friends, noticed her and felt something telling him to go talk to her. He walked up and greeted her. She smiled and said, “Hey.” Salman asked, “Did you see my Instagram account? I followed you.” She checked and realized she hadn’t noticed because she had multiple accounts. She followed him back and explained that the account he found wasn’t her main one. Salman asked why she had so many accounts, and she just smiled and said, “I just like it that way.”

They didn’t talk much at the time, but at least she had followed him back and replied to his message. That meant they had a chance to keep communicating even after school. Later that day, after going to the mini-mart with friends, Salman returned and found most students had gone home. Only a few remained, including Khadija. This time, Salman and Khadija started having a deeper conversation without even realizing it. They introduced themselves properly and talked a lot. That day, they truly understood each other better. If not for that moment, they wouldn’t have connected the way they did. And that was an unexpected bound.

Chapter 3: Deeper Bond

That evening, after returning home, Salman found himself once again drawn into a long conversation with Khadija. At first, it was casual—little fragments of laughter, stories about the day—but the chat stretched on longer than either of them had expected. The more they typed, the more they discovered pieces of themselves hidden in the other.

Khadija spoke with a quiet passion about her love for books, how they offered her entire worlds to escape into. Salman, in turn, shared his interests: documentaries, history, the kinds of things that most people around him would brush aside as boring. But he quickly noticed she wasn’t the kind of person who would thrive in long, technical discussions about computers and codes—the world he usually lived in. So, instead of burying her under the weight of his obsessions, he let those stay in the background. What mattered was finding the bridge between them, and as if by chance, they both landed on one thing they shared: art.

The discovery of that common ground lit something in Salman. That night they spoke endlessly about history, documentaries ,art and alot of other things. And in that moment, Salman felt something unusual—a sense of connection, fragile but real, a warmth he hadn’t felt in years.

Their chats became a rhythm of life. Day after day, night after night, they shared stories, jokes, interests and thoughts. Khadija had an energy about her—a willingness to give her time and attention in a way that startled Salman. He thought back to the Aura Gambit incident, the awkwardness that could have broken any fragile friendship before it began. Yet here she was, speaking to him like none of it mattered. He never asked her why. He didn’t need to. The way she stayed was answer enough.

Weeks slipped by, and graduation loomed closer. Salman knew, in the back of his mind, that it would be the last time they would meet in person. Life would scatter everyone in different directions, as it always did. But instead of clinging to dread, he kept things normal, burying the thought with every conversation.

Khadija, with her entrepreneurial mindset sells shawarma, since shawarma happened to be one of his favorite meals. When he ordered one, she promised to bring it for him on graduation day. The thought lingered with him, becoming part of the quiet anticipation of the event.

Not long before the big day, Salman asked Khadija for a picture of herself so he could draw her. She agreed. That night, while the house was silent and the rest of the world slept, he sat hunched over his desk with paper and pencil in hand. He drew her with meticulous care, pausing often, erasing, redrawing, making sure every detail resembled her. It wasn’t just a sketch. To him, it was a gift, a memory frozen in lines and shadows. He imagined her reaction when he handed it over. For Salman, drawing had always been personal. He never drew men. It was only women he found beautiful, To draw a girl meant her beauty was acknoledged by him.

The graduation day finally arrived. The air was alive with excitement, Everyone’s faces glowed with relief and joy—years of effort had finally come to an end. Salman walked into the hall, His eyes searched restlessly, looking for only one person: Khadija.

She had told him she would be late; her makeup took nearly an hour. It made Salman laugh when she said it, but in that moment, waiting for her, the minutes stretched endlessly. The ceremony came and went, speeches echoing, applause rising, the collective joy of a class reaching its final moment together. And then, outside the hall, amidst the sea of gowns and laughter, Salman finally saw her.

His excitement bubbled to the surface as he approached her. “I have a surprise for you,” he said, unable to keep the grin off his face. She tilted her head playfully, eyes bright. “Oh, let me see!” she replied. Salman pulled out the folded paper and placed it in her hands. But instead of opening it, she simply smiled and tucked it away. He didn’t understand why, but oddly, he liked it. It felt as though she was saving the moment, holding it close, making it hers in silence.

Then she handed him his shawarma, neatly wrapped. Salman laughed quietly to himself, slipping it into his pocket like a prized treasure before going off to greet parents, classmates, and old friends. Later, he called one of his homie musa aside, unwrapped the shawarma, and devoured it, every bite tasting better than the last, because of who it came from.

The day rolled into celebration. The graduating class made their way to a restaurant a few miles away. The atmosphere was alive with light and sound—RGB colors danced across the walls, music pounded through the speakers, and laughter echoed in every corner. Plates of food filled the tables, people played games, danced wildly, and captured every moment in photos. Salman wanted to speak more with Khadija, but she was often with one of her sisters. The timing wasn’t right, and the setting was too loud. Still, just being in the same room, watching her from afar, felt enough.

That night ended with joy, and as everyone returned home, Salman carried with him something deeper than simple happiness—he carried contentment.

From that day on, their friendship only grew stronger. They spoke every day, about everything and nothing. Sometimes they challenged each other with drawing competitions, sometimes they discuss about anime, and sometimes they just sent each other reels that somehow made sense only between them. Their bond deepened like roots intertwining beneath the surface, invisible but unbreakable.

They even turned their bond into a kind of game—a “side quest,” as they called it. Khadija would recommend a book for Salman to read in a week, while he would recommend an anime for her to watch. At the end of the week,so that they’d share their thoughts later on.

For Khadija, the anime was an easy task—she had always been a fan. But for Salman, the book was torture. He hated reading. The endless pages, the tiny text, the heavy words that seemed to mock him—it was everything he had always avoided. Yet when Khadija recommended The Poppy War, he didn’t refuse. He forced himself through page after page, eyes watering from the strain of reading on a screen, constantly pausing to search up difficult words. But he never quit. He had made her a promise, and more than that, he wanted to honor their bond.

Slowly, the story began to take hold of him. What started as a chore became something else. Each night, before sleep, he reads it, reading until exhaustion made his eyelids heavy. To his surprise, he began to enjoy it. The fatigue even lulled him into deeper sleep.

Meanwhile, Khadija had taken his recommendation seriously and was watching Vinland Saga, an anime Salman considered one of the best he had ever seen that year.

And in the midst of all this—between the reels, the drawings, the side quests,Salman realized something. Khadija was the first female friend he had ever acknowledged as a true friend. For the first time in his life.

Chapter 4: The Chess Date

The news came without warning.
“Salman, you’re leaving Abuja.”

His sister’s words echoed in his head, and for a moment, it felt like the world had tilted. He wasn’t ready. He wasn’t prepared. The ground beneath his life shifted too suddenly, leaving him dizzy with a mixture of disbelief and sorrow. He had been in Abuja, finishing his exams while the rest of his family had already moved back to Kano. Now the time had come to join them, and though he knew it was inevitable, he hadn’t expected it to be this soon. The realization struck him with one painful truth: he would never see Khadija again.

That night, while the city outside his window hummed faintly, he sat with his phone in his hand, heart restless. He was supposed to go swimming with Musa the next day for one last memory with his closest friend. But another longing weighed heavier, something he could not ignore. If he was truly leaving, he needed to see Khadija one more time. Not just in passing. Not casually. He needed a moment with her alone, something real, something lasting.

His fingers trembled slightly as he typed the message:
“Would you like to meet me tomorrow for a chess date? Just you and me… before I leave.”

Then came the silence. Minutes stretched endlessly. He checked his phone over and over, the anxiety knotting tighter in his chest with every passing second. The weight of no reply crushed him until exhaustion dragged his worried heart into sleep.

The next morning, light filtered through his window, and the very first thing he did was check his phone. His heart skipped—she had replied. She said yes.

Relief washed over him, but it wasn’t just relief—it was joy, sharp and overwhelming. For her, perhaps, it was just kindness, friendship, a simple gesture. But for him, it was everything. That she would choose to spend part of his last day with him… it made him feel honored, valued, seen.

He set the venue: Jabi Lake Mall. A vast, lively place where laughter mingled with the hum of shops and the breeze off the lake carried the scent of water and sky. The time was set—1:30 p.m.

Salman hurried to get ready. He bathed quickly, pulled on casual clothes, but then chaos broke out—his chessboard. His nephews had scattered the pieces, and in a frenzy he searched every corner. At last, he managed to gather almost all of them, though a few pawns were missing. Desperate but determined, he replaced them with checkers pieces, chuckling softly at the irony. It wasn’t perfect, but real friendship connections rarely were.

He arrived at the mall half an hour early. He walked slowly along the lake’s edge, the breeze cool against his face, before choosing a quiet table outside. There, with steady hands, he set up the chessboard as though preparing not for a game, but for a memory.

When her message came—“I’m on my way”—his pulse quickened.
Minutes later, her call lit up his screen.
“I’m in the mall now. Where are you?”

His voice carried a thrill he tried to disguise. “I’m outside, by the lake. Keep walking—I’ll find you.”

He rose and began moving through the crowd, eyes darting like a pirate searching for treasure. Then—he saw her. Her walk was familiar, yet everything about that moment made it new. His breath caught as his gaze lifted to her face. There she was—Khadija—her smile glowing bright. For a heartbeat, the world around him dimmed.

He raised his hand instinctively, smiling with a joy he could no longer contain. She smiled back, that radiant warmth that seemed to say I see you too.

At their table, he taught her chess. She listened attentively, surprising him with how quickly she learned. Her eyes sparkled when she grasped each rule, each move, each small strategy. In no time, they were playing a real game. Yet even as the pieces clicked against the board, Salman was more focused on the way she leaned forward in concentration, the way her smile broke through when she understood something new.

After a while Salman asked if she wanted to rematch or have some fun. She replied, “Let’s have some fun.”

They wandered into an ice cream shop. Salman ordered shawarma and ice cream, Khadija only ice cream. “I’ll pay you back,” she offered softly. He shook his head, smiling faintly, knowing he wouldn’t accept even if she tried. After all, he had invited her.

Their conversation flowed like a river—effortless, bright. They spoke of their “side quest,” of Vinland Saga, the anime he had recommended, and The Poppy War, the book she had urged him to read. But beneath the laughter, Salman felt the quiet ache of time slipping away. Every glance, every laugh—he held onto them as though they were already memories.

Later, she wanted a burger, and he walked beside her as if even that small act was sacred. They wandered through aisles, lingering over things they didn’t need, their steps slow, unhurried. For Salman, every second stretched golden, fragile, like glass he dared not break.

And when the day finally ended, when goodbyes hovered on their lips, Salman knew this: it wasn’t the chess, or the ice cream, or even the laughter that made it unforgettable. It was her. It was the way she had said yes. The way she had shown up. The way she had smiled at him.

As Khadija was leaving, Salman escorted her to the Uber where she left. Not long after, he also left the place—with a heart full of joy and contentment.

Chapter 5: Bye Bye

Both Salman and Khadija went back home after the chess date. Salman's smiles carried warmth, but his eyes held sadness. He knew it was supposed to be their last meeting before he finally leaves Abuja. Salman hated the thought of returning to Kano. Abuja felt more alive, Especially now that he had met a new friend khadija. Even though their time together had been short, the bond between them grew so strong, stronger than some of the friendships that were built for so long.

That night he packed his belongings with a heavy heart. Each fold of a shirt, each zip of the bag, made him feel like he was sealing away memories. Early the next morning he got ready and stepped out of his room, ready to leave, his sister was in the palor as they made eye contact she suddenly told him he wasn’t traveling that day. She explained she had thought he would go with her husband, but since he wasn’t prepared, they would wait until she finished her own tasks so they could leave together. Relief washed over him like a wave. Salman was thrilled he had more time in Abuja, yet also frustrated his effort and energy spent packing had all been for nothing. He sighed, dropped his bag, and returned to his room. As he lay back down, a quiet smile stretched across his face. The thought of seeing Khadija again made his chest tighten in excitement.

Soon, Khadija’s summer carnival stall for her shawarma business was coming up. Salman had thought he would never get the chance to be there, but now hope sparked again. He imagined walking through the carnival, and the taste of her shawarma warm in his hands. He even pictured himself standing by her side, helping her serve customers, sneaking little smiles between the rush of people. And gift he had carefully chosen for her: an NBA Lakers jersey. He knew how much she loved basketball, and he imagined the look on her face when she held it, the way her eyes would shine when she realized it was from him. He was ready. Ready to create one more memory with her that he could never forget.

But that night his sister crushed his plans again. She told him they would be leaving the next day. The words hit him like a punch to the chest. He stared at the packed bag by his bed, his throat tight, his heart sinking into his stomach. And though why does she always tell him about it in the worst time possibel, Rage and sadness tangled inside him why now, why , when all he wanted was just one more day? Quietly, painfully, he packed for the last time.

The next morning they rushed to catch the train. By 10:00 they reached Kaduna, and by 1:00 he was already in Kano. Yet the journey felt empty. Salman pressed his forehead against the train window, watching the fields pass by, his reflection staring back at him with a question he couldn’t escape: *When will I see her again?* He knew he had left behind not just friends, but a piece of himself in Abuja. Every memory, every glance from Khadija now pulled at his heart like invisible strings.

That night he called her. As he apologized for missing the carnival. On the other end, Khadija admitted she had been waiting, hoping he would show up. The guilt in Salman’s chest was unbearable. Yet she told him it was okay, her voice soft, forgiving. Even with distance between them, their connection felt alive, as though no miles could separate what they had built. After all, Salman and Khadija beacme online friends and chatted every single day that only sleep seprates them. She became part of his life as they enjoy each others company without even trying.

Chapter 6: Online friends: Part I

Salman and Khadija, despite being online friends, shared a bond that grew stronger each day. They chatted constantly, eager to uncover each other’s mysteries and learn more about themselves. Khadija told Salman about the many countries she had visited, including Morocco, Romania, and Atlanta, where she had even schooled. Salman, who had never been outside Nigeria except for one francophone country in Africa, listened with excitement. He was fascinated by her experiences, but what caught his interest most was when she confessed about having a crush. He begged her to tell him more, curious about the kind of guy who had captured her attention. She explained that she liked him mainly because of how he dressed. Hearing this, Salman had a sudden flashback to the day of their chess date and thought, damn, why did I dress so simple? I could have pulled off the best drip she’s ever seen. Still, he lowkey had to accepted his faith he wasn’t the type who focused much on dressing, as long as he dressed simply and comfortably.

She went on to reveal that she had two crushes at the time. When Salman asked her to describe her type, she didn’t exaggerate like most girls often did. Instead, she gave a realistic description: a dark-skinned, tall, handsome, and slightly muscular guy. Salman laughed to himself, thinking, *okay, not bad that’s literally me, I just need to hit the gym more.* Yet he brushed that thought aside. On the other hand, Khadija asked Salman about his type. He admitted he was drawn to certain rare eyes that stood out and made him unconsciously stare without reason. At that moment, he only knew one girl who had those eyes, and he couldn’t even describe them properly. Deep down though, he knew that if a guy asked, he’d probably joke and say he liked tall, older women with a nice big a$$ just like megantheestallion. Regardless, Salman and Khadija enjoyed their long-distance friendship, playing engaging games like Truth or Dare and sharing relatable reels to keep the vibe alive.

Around that time, Salman was also interested in one of his classmates, a girl Khadija surprisingly became friends with. He had liked that girl since their junior classes, drawn to her eyes the first he thought matched the rare kind he admired. Even though his feelings seemed one-sided, he couldn’t shake them off. But everything shifted when Khadija came in. She became the first female friend he was truly close to, with late-night chats, shared humor, and an unexpected bond that felt deeper than talking to a crush. Slowly, the feelings he had for his classmate disappeared. He no longer felt the urge to see her picture or chase her attention. For the first time, he realized the weight of those emotions had lifted. Without even knowing it, Khadija had broken the curse and freed him from a lingering attachment he thought he’d never let go of.

Khadija and Salman continued with their little side quests. Salman, being a passionate chess player, often dreamed of making Khadija one too. He imagined the games they would play and the friendly competitions that would strengthen their bond. Chess, to him, was more than a game it was strategy, patience, and intellect, all things he valued deeply. Khadija had always been interested in learning, but found it difficult to start on her own without guidance or someone beside her to teach. Salman often wished he could be there in person to help her. One day, Salman came up with a plan he thought would motivate her. He asked Khadija about the best book she had ever read. She told him about Chain of Gold, adding that nothing would make her happier than if he read it too. Salman, who always found joy in seeing her happy, agreed. But after hearing how many pages it had, he knew he was lowkey kinda cooked. Still, he struck a playful deal: if Khadija could last seven minutes against him in a game of chess, he would read the book. Salman was confident it would take weeks of practice for any beginner to survive that long against him. Keeping his promise, Salman searched for Chain of Gold and began reading it every night before bed. Truthfully, he didn’t enjoy it much. He had liked the first book she recommended, The Poppy War, far more, as Chain of Gold was not the kind of story most guys would normally pick up. Still, he pushed himself through nearly six hundred pages, determined to keep his word and to turn Khadija into his chess partner. So committed was he that he even asked her to block him for a week, just so he could focus more on reading— a decision that, in hindsight, felt a little rough, but showed just how far he was willing to go.
